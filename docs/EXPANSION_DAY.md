# Six-faction expansion

The local build now includes six factions, seeded maps in three sizes, functional terrain, crystal, and a terminal interface. Navigation and AI scheduling fixes are included. The combined game passes [179 regression tests](evidence/day-verification/regression.log), all 216 final ladder games finished, and six complete terminal-agent games have verified state-hash replays. [Browser checks](evidence/day-verification/BROWSER.md) exercised menu configuration, rendering, selection, movement, construction, recruitment and the crystal economy. The new factions remain uneven in the automated matches.

The original [64-game ladder](evidence/ladder-64/REPORT.md) is preserved. Later runs have separate source snapshots and results. The local decision log is `work/day-expansion/decisions.tsv`. Three malformed rows were repaired with their original bytes retained, and several later entries were reconstructed at a shared timestamp; this is an auditable decision record, not a precise action timeline.

## Maps, navigation and fairness

Seeds generate 36, 48 or 64 tile square maps. Starts, resources and terrain are rotationally symmetric. Connectivity and resource-access checks passed for 300 generated maps. Roads accelerate movement, mud and shallows slow most troops, bridges cross water, and water/cliffs block movement. Buildings require visible grass or road across their footprint.

Navigation resolves obstructed ground destinations, checks movement segments, retries narrow gaps at quarter-tile resolution and skips unnecessary intermediate waypoints to reduce crowd deadlocks. Both AIs decide on the same ticks with alternating command order, foundation placement uses headquarters-relative offsets, and simultaneous lethal attacks resolve together. These fixes reduce specific ordering defects without eliminating every side effect. Opposing Gravecallers still claim shared corpses in entity order.

Crystal pays for special units and towers; wood and ore fund the opening troops. Home deposits are limited and further deposits encourage expansion. Gathering, deposits, costs, UI and AI use the same economy rules.

## Tideborn and Automata implementation

Tideborn units traverse mud and shallows at 1.1 times their base speed; ordinary units slow to 0.72 and 0.65 respectively. Deep water and cliffs remain impassable for all factions. Tidecallers restore 35 HP within five tiles and grant 25% movement speed for six seconds, with a 20-second cooldown.

Automata workers, melee, ranged and special units carry 12, 45, 35 and 45 shield respectively. Damage consumes shield before health. Shields recharge at four points per second after six seconds without damage. Ward Engines restore 24 shield within five tiles, on a 20-second cooldown, and automatically respond to damaged friendly shields.

Faction portraits and selected-unit/building artwork are crops of Blender renders. The ImageGen reference is retained in `art/reference/tideborn-and-automata.png`; production models and actions are generated by `art/blender/world_expansion.py`. The menu offers all six factions, three map sizes and a numeric seed. The HUD displays crystal costs, current shields and Surge status. The battlefield draws seeded terrain and uses map dimensions for camera, fog and command bounds. Each match loads only its two factions' animation atlases.

## Terminal interface

The [terminal protocol](TERMINAL_AGENTS.md) supports start, observe, command, advance and result operations. Player observations hide enemy banks, orders and production queues, and retain last-observed resource amounts under fog. The [final six CLI matches](evidence/terminal-v6/summary.json) cover every faction, all map sizes, both player sides and two seeds. All six finished; the example agent won three and lost three. Every recorded request reproduced its post-request simulation state hash. This checks simulation state, not response bytes or separately stored observation/runtime state. Replays require the same simulation version.

## Navigation follow-up

The first six-faction ladder flagged movement stalls despite the earlier focused navigation tests. Replaying small seed 4127, Orcs versus Fairies, exposed a legal one-tile gap between the enemy barracks and HQ. After collision padding, the opening fell between every half-tile grid center. A focused regression failed before the change and passes after it. Failed coarse searches now retry on a quarter-tile grid. The original ladder source and results remain separate from the follow-up run.

## Final verification and remaining concerns

The [final ladder](evidence/six-factions-final-v6/REPORT.md) includes all 36 ordered matchups on three sizes and two seeds: 216 completed games, no 45-minute timeouts, a 10:23 median and 30:47 maximum. Dwarves versus Orcs finished 7–5 and Orcs versus Undead 7–5. These use different maps and scheduling from the baseline, so they are not controlled comparisons of unit stats.

Automata finished 5–55 and Tideborn 21–39 across cross-faction games. Mirror games favored side 0 by 24–12; cross-faction games favored it 91–89. One large-map Dwarf mirror produced a suspected movement stall. No sampled economy violations or non-finite/out-of-bounds coordinates were found. The coordinate check does not establish collision correctness; focused navigation regressions exercise those cases. These deterministic samples do not establish competitive balance.

The packed assets include 70 manifest entries, 4728 animation frames and 72 atlas pages. [Unit samples](evidence/day-art/units.png), [building samples](evidence/day-art/buildings.png) and [environment samples](evidence/day-art/terrain.png) were visually inspected. That review covers four action samples in direction zero for each new unit, idle/construction/death for each new building, and seven new environment assets. Structural validation checks all animation coverage and image differences, not artistic quality in every frame.

All atlases together decode to 737.9 MiB, but matches load only the participating factions. The Automata–Fairies browser match loaded 24 pages, about 253.65 MiB. There is no new controlled 100-unit performance benchmark; momentary browser FPS is not a substitute. Browser play is integration evidence, while full roster, abilities and outcomes across all pairings are covered by simulation and CLI runs.
