# Original-goal acceptance audit

This audit evaluates the original Orcs vs Fairies objective. It does not add equal faction win rates, a victory with every faction on every revision, a guarantee that every match lasts 10–15 minutes, or pixel-identical Blender renders. The current production revision is `index-9PMVYv2m.js`. This audit changed no game source or production files.

`performance-hold.json` and its provenance bind the latest maintained 100-unit measurement to this revision. The collector reports a valid sample, no invalid reasons and 100 living, visible, on-screen units for both minima and maxima.

## Visual acceptance and its limits

The audit directly inspected `art/blender/raw/units/roster-contact.png` and `directions-contact.png`. Orcs have broad bodies, armor and red accents; Fairies have slender bodies, veined wings and leaf/purple/gold accents. Roles have equipment and silhouette cues at exported gameplay scale. `VISUAL_REVIEW.md` and earlier normal matches add actual battlefield observation of movement, attacks, construction, mixed armies, fog and commanded units. Consistent scene lighting and generated meshes support that result. Atlas counts or nonempty alpha alone cannot prove it.

The exact rear Wardrum view hides its front drum. Its bright crest remains distinct from the worker's flatter headband, while side/front directions show the drum and beaters. The rear view is a weaker role cue, and should not be described as equally clear in every direction. The inspected sheet still provides a recognizable unit silhouette and faction identity; the recorded normal play does not report a role-recognition failure that prevented commanding it. This is a qualified acceptance of gameplay-size recognition, not a claim that the limitation disappears because it was documented.

Trees and roofs overlap nearby units through ordinary depth ordering. Selected or damaged visible units receive health indicators in the top overlay, above the world sprites. Ground-based drag selection, groups and F2 remain available. Recorded matches commanded workers at trees and troops around bases, rather than showing a battlefield made unusable by occlusion. That combination supports clear, usable battlefield presentation with ordinary overlap. It does not promise that every full-health, unselected unit remains visible behind all scenery. A specific observed case that hides required interaction beyond these cues would require correction; none is established in the retained visual record. Documentation alone would not excuse such a failure.

The `flowers` asset loads but is not placed by the map renderer. It is an unused decorative export, not missing playable terrain. Avoid claiming all 15 exported decorations appear in the map.

## Target qualifications and conclusion

The current normal match lasted 805.05 seconds, within the requested 600–900-second range. This supports the match-length target; it is not a promise about every strategy, skill level or outcome.

The latest benchmark sampled 3,512 frames over 60,008 ms after 5,000 ms warmup: 58.52552992934275 FPS. Median frame interval was 17.1 ms, p95 18.7 ms and p99 19.4 ms. Viewport, canvas and drawing buffer were each 1920 x 1080, with exactly 100 living/visible/on-screen units throughout. Art was loaded, audio unmuted and simulation running during sampling. The reported browser DPR was 1.5; drawing-buffer dimensions, rather than a DPR assumption, establish the requested pixel workload. The collector then paused. The original wording asks to measure against a 60 FPS target, so the measurement requirement is met while the performance target itself is missed by about 1.47 FPS. Delivery must disclose that shortfall.

No original deliverable remains identified as missing or unproven after applying the evidence and qualifications above. This finding does not certify perfect balance, universal visual visibility, every optional input path, every match duration, or 60 FPS performance. Those are kept distinct from the requested playable milestone and its explicit measurement obligation. Final task reporting should preserve the measured shortfall and known limits without adding open-ended balance work as a new acceptance condition.
