I’d prioritize save/load, stronger AI, distinct faction mechanics for cavalry and siege, and match replays. Here are 100 ideas for the current six-faction RTS.

### Combat and tactics

1. Army formations: arrange troops into lines, wedges, squares or loose skirmish groups.
2. Flanking bonuses: reward attacks against an enemy’s sides and rear.
3. High-ground advantages: elevated positions improve vision and ranged attacks.
4. Morale: isolated or badly damaged squads can retreat or surrender.
5. Directional shields: shield units protect troops standing behind them.
6. Cover: rocks, ruins and buildings reduce incoming ranged damage.
7. Siege friendly fire: careless artillery placement can hurt your own army.
8. Cavalry charges: build impact through movement, with pikes stopping the charge.
9. Capturable siege engines: defeat their crews and claim the equipment.
10. Ambush orders: conceal troops and set conditions for revealing them.

### Economy and settlements

11. Forestry: workers plant groves that become future wood supplies.
12. Deep mines: invest in expensive structures to extend depleted ore deposits.
13. Crystal overcharging: extract faster at the risk of damaging the extractor.
14. Trade caravans: transport resources between settlements for income.
15. Neutral markets: exchange resources at prices that respond to demand.
16. Supply raids: attack caravans or storage buildings to steal resources.
17. Battlefield salvage: recover materials from destroyed buildings and siege engines.
18. Regional warehouses: stockpile resources locally and organize deliveries.
19. Settlement specialization: develop expansion bases into mining, military or research centers.
20. Resource contracts: complete deliveries for neutral settlements in exchange for rewards.

### Faction identity

21. Orc war chants: spend Fury on temporary offensive or defensive army buffs.
22. Orc trophy standards: victorious troops establish banners that strengthen nearby allies.
23. Fairy illusion swapping: exchange a real unit’s position with its illusion.
24. Fairy enchanted groves: cultivated forests conceal allies and mislead enemy scouts.
25. Dwarf tunnels: transfer squads between constructed underground entrances.
26. Dwarf workshop modifications: choose different ammunition or attachments for artillery.
27. Undead corpse wagons: collect bodies and deliver them to Gravecallers.
28. Undead necropolises: convert captured territory into ground that sustains summoned troops.
29. Tideborn water shaping: create temporary shallows, mud or flooded defensive approaches.
30. Automata power networks: connected structures share shields and power specialized defenses.

### Units and progression

31. Branching technology trees: mutually exclusive research choices change your army’s strengths.
32. Faction-specific cavalry mechanics: give each mounted unit a distinct battlefield role.
33. Faction-specific siege mechanics: distinguish artillery through targeting, deployment and ammunition.
34. Expanded military research: add upgrades for ranged troops, cavalry and siege.
35. Veteran units: surviving battles grants experience and visible distinctions.
36. Unit promotions: choose a specialization when a veteran reaches a milestone.
37. Heroes: recruit named commanders with limited, faction-specific abilities.
38. Equipment artifacts: recover items that modify a hero or specialist.
39. Signal beacons: connect distant outposts for shared vision and invasion warnings.
40. Combat engineers: construct temporary bridges, barricades and field repairs.

### Maps and the world

41. Procedural biomes: generate deserts, marshes, snowy valleys and dense forests.
42. Day and night: change visibility and the usefulness of different units.
43. Weather: rain, fog and wind affect movement, sight and projectiles.
44. Destructible bridges: cut routes or isolate advancing armies.
45. Forest fires: spread through trees, destroying resources and opening routes.
46. Seasonal maps: freeze lakes or change river crossings during a match.
47. Monster dens: defeat neutral creatures for resources or territory.
48. Neutral villages: earn loyalty to recruit local defenders or obtain supplies.
49. Relic sites: capture locations that grant persistent strategic bonuses.
50. Underground battlefields: connect surface bases with contested caves and tunnels.

### AI and practice

51. Honest difficulty settings: vary AI decision quality without hidden resource bonuses.
52. AI personalities: choose opponents that rush, fortify, expand or raid.
53. AI opening strategies: opponents use recognizable builds with exploitable weaknesses.
54. Scouting-based counters: AI adapts its army to enemies it has observed.
55. Coordinated allied AI: computer teammates organize attacks and divide responsibilities.
56. AI retreat and regrouping: opponents preserve troops and return with reinforcements.
57. Allied AI directives: request defense, scouting, resource support or attacks.
58. Visible handicap settings: explicitly adjust starting resources, income or population limits.
59. Practice coach: explain missed opportunities using information available to the player.
60. Agent tournament dashboard: run terminal-agent competitions and inspect their matches.

### Multiplayer and game modes

61. Online 1v1 matches: play against another human.
62. Team battles: support 2v2, 3v3 and 4v4.
63. Cooperative skirmishes: human teammates fight coordinated computer opponents.
64. Live spectators: watch matches with optional delayed viewing.
65. Ranked seasons: track competitive results with periodic rating resets.
66. Custom lobbies: configure victory rules, starting ages, resources and unit restrictions.
67. King of the hill: hold a contested central location to win.
68. Relic victory: collect and defend enough relics instead of destroying every stronghold.
69. Survival mode: defend against increasingly difficult attack waves.
70. Army draft: take turns selecting units or technologies before the match.

### Campaign and scenarios

71. Six faction campaigns: give each faction its own characters and strategic problems.
72. World conquest: fight connected battles on a persistent regional map.
73. Branching missions: earlier decisions change later objectives and allies.
74. Persistent armies: surviving units carry experience into the next mission.
75. Campaign diplomacy: negotiate alliances, tribute and temporary truces.
76. Escort missions: protect caravans, refugees or moving siege equipment.
77. Fortress defense missions: survive a siege with limited reinforcements.
78. Stealth missions: infiltrate territory using scouts, illusions and restricted armies.
79. Boss encounters: fight creatures requiring positioning and coordinated abilities.
80. Tactical puzzles: solve short scenarios with a fixed army and limited resources.
