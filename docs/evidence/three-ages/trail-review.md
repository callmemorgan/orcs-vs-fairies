# Cross-model review of the three-age decision trail

Reviewed by GPT-5.6 Sol. I compared `work/three-ages/decisions.tsv` with the September 16 goal run in `rollout-2026-09-04T20-34-31-01a06fa1-d2c1-7663-a270-fed0750a9131.jsonl` and the evidence under `work/three-ages`. This review covers the trail and its evidence, not the implementation as a whole. The run was still finishing its benchmark, documentation, commits, and preview when this review began.

## Findings

1. The 06:04 regression row cites a failed suite as a successful checkpoint. `work/three-ages/tests-v2.log` says `Test Files 1 failed | 14 passed` after the 36 matches completed. Its 232 individual tests passed, but the suite failed because the aggregate building assertion still rejected walls and gates. The transcript shows the assertion was corrected after that run. The row should say the run exposed an obsolete assertion and was not green, then point a later row to the successful rerun. As written, “232 tests passed; obsolete aggregate assertion corrected and strengthened” hides the failing suite and claims a correction the cited log does not contain.

2. The 06:34 final verification row joins two claims but cites only one of their proofs. `work/three-ages/tests-v6.log` proves that 16 test files and 237 tests passed. The claim that all six factions reached Citadel Age and used all roles across 36 pairings comes from `work/three-ages/regression-summary.json`. The row needs both paths. The summary also states that all 36 games use one default-map seed, so the row should preserve that limit rather than let “full-match coverage” imply broad balance or map coverage.

3. The trail stops short of recording the final artwork checks. The 05:55, 06:00, and 06:12 artwork rows all end with a batch running or frames regenerating. The completed evidence is stronger and should have its own checkpoint: `frame-validation.json` checked 3,456 new unit frames with no missing or clipped frames, `asset-validation-final.json` passed 8,250 frames across 100 assets, and `gates-final.log` regenerated all faction walls and gates. Without a final row, a reader following only the trail sees three unfinished artwork states and cannot tell that the work completed.

4. Several artwork rows point to files that do not contain the stated observation. `art-tight-sample.log` records Blender renders but does not record that a 192-pixel canvas clipped the pike or that 224 pixels fixed it. `death-directions.log` records the renders but does not contain the asserted 26-pixel minimum margin; the later full-frame report records a 23-pixel minimum. The 06:37 gate row points to the generator source, which proves the geometry changed but does not preserve the visual inspection that motivated it. These decisions are credible from the transcript, but their evidence pointers are not enough for someone auditing the trail later. A small machine-readable bounds report and saved before/after gate images would make the claims durable.

5. The larger-map objective is only partly proved. The 06:06 row admits that its 61 focused tests ran before scout integration, and it cites `src/core/maps.ts`, not the test log. Later small, large, and huge matches completed, but no artifact shows that the AI discovered, contested, or economically used the new flank camps. The huge match also lacks gates on both sides, and one side of the small match never produced siege before the game ended. Those outcomes do not invalidate the feature, but they are weaker than “make larger maps worth exploring through contested resources and room for expansion.” Keep the completed match evidence and add a direct observation or test for scout arrival and expansion use at a flank camp.

6. The 05:47 foundation row overstates what its checkpoint had verified. Its three focused tests cover paid timed advancement, prerequisites, and duplicate research prevention. They do not exercise the military damage and armor effects named in the decision. Those effects were tested later in `tests/progression.test.ts` and included in the final suite, so the work is now covered; the early row should either omit “military effects” or cite the later focused combat check in a separate row.

7. The 06:05 fortification row mixes directly tested behavior with a broader AI claim. `tests/progression.test.ts` verifies expansion survival, gate passage, refusal to close on a unit, age unlocks, and pike damage. It does not prove that walls join visually or that the AI builds effective “defensive screens.” `regression-summary.json` proves every faction built wall and gate types across the 36-game sample, but it does not assess placement quality. Record those as separate facts and treat screen quality as visual or match evidence.

8. The 06:22 specialist-age row has no result evidence. `src/core/content.ts` shows the intended age values, while the result mentions updated fixtures rather than a passing command or log. `age-gates-tests.log` and `rally-all-roles.log` are the relevant proof and should be cited. This matters because the specialist gate was a late behavior change after earlier match bundles had already been generated.

9. The browser work and final delivery were absent from the trail when reviewed. The transcript and artifacts show a normal huge-map Orc match ending in defeat at 6:33, a technology-tree layout screenshot, worker rally and Town Age interaction, and 120 telemetry samples. The Citadel interaction was interrupted by the loss, and there is no trail row yet for browser gate operation, human expansion, Citadel recruitment, the final benchmark, a playable preview, or commits. These may be added after this review, but completion should not be claimed until the trail points to the final browser, performance, preview, and commit evidence.

10. The match evidence reveals balance and memory risks that deserve an explicit limitation. In the 36-game single-seed sample, Orcs finished 11–1 while Automata and Dwarves each finished 2–10. This does not block functional verification, but it is too skewed to support a claim of healthy composition choices. The packed atlas set also decodes to 1,448.63 MiB in full; selective loading limits a two-faction match, but the final browser benchmark needs to record loaded pages, decoded memory, frame rate, and warnings before the preview is treated as verified.

The remaining flags are limited and are stated in the final report:

1. No single full-suite run covers the exact final 239-test commit. The complete run passed 237 tests, then two tests were added and the 15 progression/interface tests passed against the final source. `docs/THREE_AGES.md` discloses this accurately, and the source fingerprint prevents ambiguity about the reviewed tree, but `tests-final.log` alone is not proof that every test passes on commit `d35bad8`.

2. If the evidence directory must stand alone without the transcript, preserve a compact before/after browser-state record or cite one alongside the screenshot. The gate open/close and wall join claims do have durable screenshot and state evidence.

3. The AI reached the new flank camps but did not mine or expand at them in the recorded large match. The added focused test proves that a player-controlled worker can travel to a camp, build an expansion headquarters, gather ore, and deliver it locally. This establishes the map and economy path, while consistent AI economic use of distant camps remains unfinished product behavior rather than a missing disclosure.

4. Browser play did not reach Citadel Age or exercise every late-game unit. The human match ended in defeat at 6:33. Simulation, complete AI matches, and terminal replay cover the late progression, and the report makes the browser limit explicit. A human browser victory or every-unit browser pass should not be inferred from the current package.

These are measured and documented limits, not unsupported claims. They should remain prominent in handoff text because they affect tuning and lower-memory hardware.
