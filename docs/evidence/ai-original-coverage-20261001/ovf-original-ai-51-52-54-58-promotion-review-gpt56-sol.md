The running-simulation cases compare easy, normal, and hard with identical starting resources, troops, terrain, and income factors; apply identical gather orders and receive equal income; and show different decision counts at the same tick. Policy tests also prove easy hesitation and that difficulty profiles contain decision, counter, queue, retreat, and regroup values rather than income or resource factors. Requirement 52, “choose opponents that rush, fortify, expand or raid,” is covered. The native DOM control tests choose each of the four named personalities and emit the mapped configuration. Running AI cases then make real first commitments: rush and raid spend on a barracks, expand spends on a depot, and fortify gathers crystal before spending on a tower. The default-only 108-game matrix is not being used as personality proof.

Requirement 54, “AI adapts its army to enemies it has observed,” is covered. A visible cavalry unit causes an actual spear recruitment queue, and the observation remains available after the cavalry enters fog. A paired hidden-army case changes the unobserved enemy role and health while producing identical AI orders and empty observation memory. Focused policy cases also prove observation expiry and exclusion of workers and illusions. Together these establish adaptation caused by observed military composition rather than hidden state.

Requirement 55, “computer teammates organize attacks and divide responsibilities,” is covered. A running simulation keeps a distinct scout outside the attack force and launches both allied owners’ approved armies in the same tick despite different AI cadences. Coordinator cases assign scouting and expansion to different owners, give defense priority, reassign dead or removed responsibilities, and form a combined attack below either owner’s solo threshold. These are direct coordination and responsibility-division behaviors; a new natural match or browser run is not part of the original clause.

Requirement 56, “opponents preserve troops and return with reinforcements,” is covered at the clause’s regroup and reintegration scope. The focused running case gives a wounded fighter a retreat order, keeps it in retreat while a real paid recruit trains, and releases it from retreat only after the recruit survives and reaches the rally. Companion cases prevent a dead paid recruit or a raised unit from satisfying regrouping and show withdrawal from a visible numerical threat. Once the retreat record is removed, the preserved fighter returns to the ordinary tactical army with the reinforcement present. The case does not assert a later renewed enemy attack. Such an assertion would strengthen architecture coverage, but the original wording does not require it.
