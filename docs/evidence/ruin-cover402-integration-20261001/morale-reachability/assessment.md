## What the morale code does

The original requirement is "isolated or badly damaged squads can retreat or surrender." The two uses of "or" matter. Isolation and damage are alternative situations; the sentence does not require every unit meeting either condition to flee immediately, nor require both retreat and surrender from each situation. A separate squad-state object is not required to control a group of combat units. A proof with two living nearby survivors avoids relying on that terminology.

`src/core/tactics.ts:137-138` subtracts `damage / maxHp * 65` from morale. `simulation.ts:488` calls it for ordinary resolved damage. `tactics.ts:140-142` subtracts 12 for each nearby allied unit death, and `simulation.ts:437` calls this from the ordinary death path. These are real combat consequences. Neither operation requires isolation. Dead allies affect living units of the same team and level within six tiles.

`tactics.ts:149-152` counts support within 4.5 tiles. Support restores morale, capped at four points per second. Without support, a wounded unit with visible enemies loses four points per second; a healthy isolated unit with visible enemies loses 0.4. Wounded here means health below 65%. `tactics.ts:160` starts retreat when morale is below 22 and a visible enemy is nearby. It does not check `support === 0` or require the wounded condition at this final step. Nearby survivors therefore do not prevent retreat once combat damage and casualties overcome their recovery.

Ordinary unshielded health loss alone cannot take a surviving unit from 100 morale below 22: losing almost all its initial health removes less than 65 morale, leaving more than 35. Support also restores morale. A grouped low-health unit with no casualties or previous morale loss can therefore stay. That is a balance choice in the current logic, and does not establish failure of the broader "badly damaged squads can" wording: nearby casualties independently lower survivors' morale. A squad suffering wounds and losses has a supported retreat route. If the intended requirement instead means that health loss alone must make any supported group retreat, that stronger rule is not present, but the original sentence does not state it.

Surrender is a separate alternative. `tactics.ts:153-156` requires morale at most ten plus at least three eligible nearby enemy captors in three sectors with opponents on opposite sides. This added surround condition is consistent with "can surrender." `simulation.ts:551` applies retreat movement or the transferred ownership through the ordinary update loop; `simulation.ts:570` resolves combat before the next tick's morale update.
